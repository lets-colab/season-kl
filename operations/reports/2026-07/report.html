Three findings

1

Traffic collapsed — and it isn't a bug

Facebook supplied 90.2% of all pre-collapse traffic (1,986 of 2,202 sessions) and retained 0.2% after. Three checks rule out a technical cause: tracking still records events daily, non-Facebook sources degraded gracefully rather than zeroing, and the break predates the next code change by three days. This is a distribution failure on a single unowned channel.

2

The pipeline captures perfectly and converts nothing

51 passes issued, 51 synced to Notion, zero redeemed. The proof chain terminates at “Supabase Synced” and has never once reached “Door Verified,” because the door check-in front-end was never built. The business cannot currently prove that a single online lead became a paying guest.

3

The last two weeks shipped into a dead funnel

DM attribution, tracking links, and one-tap IG Story sharing all landed 15–16 July — after traffic had stopped. ShareClick and ShareCompleted have fired once each, on 6 July, before the feature that generates them existed. These are built, deployed, and entirely unproven.

Data & CRM Operations

Sync defective

Revised 8 Aug after Notion access. This section previously read "70 of 70 records synced — the strongest asset in the business." That came from Supabase's own sync_status, which records that a write was attempted. Reconciled against what actually landed in Notion:

  • Supabase holds 51 passes. Notion holds 77 rows — but only 45 distinct pass IDs
  • 30 duplicate rows — the same pass written repeatedly
  • 6 passes never arrived — marked synced, absent from Notion
  • Probable cause: notion-retry (v20) retrying without an idempotency key. 20 deployments is the signature of a function patched repeatedly without the root cause being found
  • Fault is isolated: the Guest CRM holds exactly 45 rows, matching the distinct count. The CRM is clean
  • Still sound: lead_safetynet caught 2 submissions that failed the primary path. Intake works — reconciliation was what never existed

Door check-in — correction

Built, never opened

The report said this was "never built." Half right. There's no website QR flow, but Notion has a fully-built door check-in database and a working form — required fields for guest, night, source, group size, verification method, proof status, owner; a QR / Pass Code option; three views; relations to passes, CRM, events and weekly reports.

Its contents: one row, created 1 July, every field null.

This changes the top recommendation. Building a QR flow is no longer the fastest route to a redemption number — opening the form that already exists is. Staff could record verified attendance this Friday with zero engineering. The blocker is an operations assignment, not a build task.

Product & Engineering

Operational

17 PRs merged. Six of them fought a single adversary — an org policy blocking unpinned GitHub Actions — and won with a runtime mode-detecting deploy pipeline. Eight pages live in four languages.

  • Missing: door QR check-in — the direct cause of zero verified attendance
  • Missing: any alerting; a 98% traffic drop went unnoticed for 27 days
  • Orphaned: a Netlify integration fails on every commit with no config in the repo. Production is unaffected (GitHub Pages), but a permanently red check trains reviewers to ignore CI — the same blindness that hid the traffic collapse

Done — read-only

Assessment

What went right: the engineering output is real. 17 PRs, 10 live automations, a working safety-net that caught 2 submissions the primary path dropped, a multilingual site, and a deploy pipeline hardened against a hostile org policy. The Notion CRM is more sophisticated than the strategy document claims — a full proof chain, door verification schema, and working forms, all built.

A correction this report has to make about itself: its first draft called the sync layer "the strongest asset in the business" on the strength of a 100% figure. That figure was the sender grading its own homework. Reconciled against the receiver, 30 rows are duplicates and 6 passes are missing. The lesson generalises — every number here that a system reports about itself needs a second source, and the ones that had one held up.

What went wrong: the business built a high-quality machine and stopped feeding it. Traffic died on 12 July and nobody noticed for weeks. And when a real campaign finally ran — 152 DMs across two weeks — it went around the tracker built for it five days earlier, so the only durable assets from it are two rate quotes and a list of display names.

The synthesis: July's engineering was spent on features that only pay off downstream of traffic — attribution, sharing, tracking — while the upstream dependency failed silently and the downstream proof was never built. The middle of the funnel is excellent. Both ends are open.

What credibility requires us to say: we cannot currently prove a single guest walked through the door because of this system. Everything else here is real, verified, and reproducible — but that one gap makes all of it provisional. Closing it is worth more than any other item on the roadmap.
